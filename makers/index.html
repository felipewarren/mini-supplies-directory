Independent Canadian miniature makers who sell online

Last checked: November 8, 2026. Each listing was checked against the maker's own website. Stock, prices and shipping change, so confirm with the maker before you order.

Many of the best 1:12 supplies in Canada come from one- or two-person studios rather than stores. This page gathers the independent makers and small cottage sellers we've confirmed so far, wherever they are in Canada, so you can buy Canadian and skip cross-border brokerage. Most sell through Etsy, a contact form or a quote request instead of a full online store, so read each listing for how ordering works. For walk-in shops, see the Canada page and the province pages in the menu.

Disclosure: no listing on this site is paid. The person who runs this site also runs Mini & Maple, which is listed below and clearly labelled.

MiniMinutia — Edmonton, AB

Tina MacDonald has been a miniaturist for more than 45 years and is the workshop coordinator of the Miniature Enthusiasts of Edmonton club. Her shop has been on Etsy since 2016 and, according to her website, has nearly 18,000 sales and over 650 products in several scales. She calls it the "Home Depot" for quarter-scalers: wallpaper (including brick, stone and kids' patterns), gingerbread trim, shingles, wooden flooring, doors and windows, and laser-cut kits in 1:12, 1:24 and 1:48. She also sells handmade baskets and wheel-thrown pottery. Her website has free tutorials on book nooks, shingling, basswood flooring, stencils and shadow boxes. Email: tina@camacdonald.com.

Source: miniminutia.com (links to its Etsy shop) · checked 2026-11-08

Fantastic Minis — Alberta

A one-person studio run by Kristy Wipf that makes handcrafted dollhouses, furniture, trees, mini food, gifts and other miniature supplies, and says it uses no 3D printing and no mass production. It takes custom commissions and quote requests. There's no storefront: it offers online studio support and private appointments by request, and you ask for delivery details and pricing on custom work. Phone: 780-554-4046 (Monday–Friday, 8 am–5 pm Mountain time). Email: kwipf47@gmail.com.

Source: fantasticminis.ca, contact, miniature supplies · checked 2026-11-08

Nasus Miniatures — Truro, NS

Susan Mattinson has made miniatures since 2018 and specializes in threadcraft such as knitting, micro crochet and embroidery. She has been a member of the International Guild of Miniature Artisans (IGMA) since 2018 and competed on the TV show "Best in Miniature." She sells through an Etsy shop and also offers patterns, video tutorials and free resources. If you live in or near Truro, she says you can message her to arrange delivery or pickup. We didn't check prices or shipping on the Etsy shop itself, so look there before you order.

Source: nasusminiatures.com, about, Etsy shop · checked 2026-11-08

Another World Miniatures — Western Canada

A small cottage business, started in 2009, that has brought dollhouse miniature supplies to shows in Western Canada; its owners say they belong to the Saskatoon Miniature Society. It isn't a single-craft studio: it sells findings, accessories, quick kits, Made in Canada hand-woven baskets, beaded jewelry pieces, silks and fine cottons, silk ribbon in over 70 shades in 2 mm and 4 mm widths, and "Mini-Stash Builder" packs of small bits like wood, lace and trim. There's no shopping cart: you send an order inquiry through the contact form with your mailing address, and they email the total with shipping and a PayPal invoice. Prices are in Canadian dollars, and it ships to Canada and the continental United States.

Check first: the contact page still mentions COVID-19, so ask before you count on an item being in stock.

Source: anotherworldminiatures.ca, about, contact · checked 2026-11-08

Mini & Maple — Québec City, QC (site owner's shop)

Handmade, laser-cut 1:12 supplies for miniature artists. The shop also sells personalized engraved maple gifts. It's online only, with no pickup, and ships within Canada only.

Source: miniandmaple.square.site

Tips for buying from small makers

  • Many makers sell on Etsy, where shipping is set per shop. On small parts, compare the shipping cost with the item price before you check out, and combine items from one maker into one order when you can.
  • Handmade pieces and commissions take time. Ask about lead time and shipping before you pay for custom work.
  • Not every "miniature" is 1:12. Check the scale before mixing a maker's pieces with dollhouse furniture from elsewhere.
  • Clubs and shows are where many of these makers sell in person. See the Events page, and read how to avoid scams before paying a seller you don't know.
